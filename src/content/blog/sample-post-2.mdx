Matrices/Determinants are among the **highest ROI** CUET topics because:
- the syllabus is compact,
- question-types repeat,
- and many problems have **1–2 step shortcuts**.

This post is written “MCQ-first”: fast methods, option-elimination, and the traps that cost marks.

---

## What CUET asks most (patterns you should expect)

1. **Basic matrix operations** (addition, multiplication, transpose)
2. **Types of matrices** (diagonal, scalar, symmetric, skew-symmetric)
3. **Determinant evaluation** (2×2 and 3×3)
4. **Determinant properties** (row/column operations)
5. **Inverse of 2×2 / concept of inverse**
6. **Solving 2-variable systems** (matrix method / Cramer’s rule style)

> ChampionsPrep Rule: If a determinant looks ugly, it’s **asking** for a row/column operation.

---

## Non-negotiable formulas (memorise these cold)

### 1) Determinant of 2×2
For \(\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{bmatrix}\),
\[
|A| = ad - bc
\]

### 2) Inverse of 2×2 (exists only if det ≠ 0)
\[
A^{-1} = \frac{1}{ad-bc}\begin{bmatrix}d&-b\\-c&a\end{bmatrix}
\]

### 3) Golden properties you’ll use daily
- \( (AB)^T = B^T A^T \)
- \( (AB)^{-1} = B^{-1}A^{-1} \) (when inverses exist)
- \( |AB| = |A|\,|B| \)
- \( |A^T| = |A| \)

## Determinant shortcuts (this is where the marks are)

### Tip Card 1 — Create zeros fast
**Trigger:** 3×3 determinant with messy numbers  
**Do this:** use **Row/Column operation**:  
\(R_2 \to R_2 - kR_1\) or \(C_3 \to C_3 + kC_1\)

**Why it works:** adding a multiple of one row/column to another **does not change** determinant.

**Watch out:** swapping two rows/columns flips the sign.

---

### Tip Card 2 — Expand along the “zero row/column”
**Trigger:** one row/column has 2 zeros  
**Do this:** cofactor expand there.

**Why it works:** you instantly reduce to 2×2.

---

### Tip Card 3 — Spot determinant = 0 in 2 seconds
det = 0 if:
- two rows/columns are identical
- two rows/columns are proportional
- one row/column is a linear combination of others

---

### Tip Card 4 — Skew-symmetric “odd order” trick (high-value MCQ fact)
If \(A\) is skew-symmetric and of **odd** order (3×3), then \(|A|=0\).

(Useful because CUET loves “true/false style” MCQs.)

---

## Matrix multiplication: the only shortcut that matters

### “Column scaling” trick
If \(B\) is diagonal, \(AB\) scales columns of \(A\).

Example:  
\(B=\text{diag}(1,2)\) means: column 2 of \(A\) becomes **twice**.

This avoids full multiplication in many MCQs.

---

## Solving 2-variable linear equations (fastest CUET route)

For:
\[
a_1x + b_1y = c_1 \\
a_2x + b_2y = c_2
\]

Use determinants:
\[
\Delta = \begin{vmatrix}a_1&b_1\\a_2&b_2\end{vmatrix} = a_1b_2-a_2b_1
\]
\[
x = \frac{\begin{vmatrix}c_1&b_1\\c_2&b_2\end{vmatrix}}{\Delta},\quad
y = \frac{\begin{vmatrix}a_1&c_1\\a_2&c_2\end{vmatrix}}{\Delta}
\]

**MCQ benefit:** often you only need \(\Delta\)’s sign/non-zero.

---

## Common traps (the 5 mistakes that repeat)

1. Mixing up **row** operation vs **column** operation effects
2. Forgetting sign change after swapping rows/cols
3. Using inverse formula without checking det ≠ 0
4. Multiplying matrices in wrong order (AB ≠ BA generally)
5. Transpose of product order mistake: \((AB)^T \neq A^TB^T\)

## Mini Practice Set (CUET-style MCQs)

### Q1
If \(A=\begin{bmatrix}1&2\\3&4\end{bmatrix}\), then \(|A|\) equals:
- A) 2
- B) -2
- C) 10
- D) -10

### Q2
If \(|A|=3\) and \(|B|=-2\), then \(|AB|\) equals:
- A) -6
- B) 6
- C) -1
- D) 1

### Q3
\(|A^T|\) equals:
- A) \(|A|\)
- B) \(-|A|\)
- C) \(|A|^T\)
- D) depends on A

### Q4
For \(A=\begin{bmatrix}2&1\\5&3\end{bmatrix}\), \(A^{-1}\) equals:
- A) \(\begin{bmatrix}3&-1\\-5&2\end{bmatrix}\)
- B) \(\begin{bmatrix}3&1\\5&2\end{bmatrix}\)
- C) \(\frac12\begin{bmatrix}3&-1\\-5&2\end{bmatrix}\)
- D) Inverse does not exist

### Q5
If two rows of a 3×3 determinant are proportional, the determinant is:
- A) 1
- B) -1
- C) 0
- D) Cannot be determined

### Q6
If \(B=\text{diag}(1,2)\) and \(A=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{bmatrix}\), then \(AB\) equals:
- A) \(\begin{bmatrix}a&2b\\c&2d\end{bmatrix}\)
- B) \(\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\2c&2d\end{bmatrix}\)
- C) \(\begin{bmatrix}2a&b\\2c&d\end{bmatrix}\)
- D) \(\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\c&d\end{bmatrix}\)

### Q7
Which statement is always true (when inverses exist)?
- A) \((AB)^{-1}=A^{-1}B^{-1}\)
- B) \((AB)^{-1}=B^{-1}A^{-1}\)
- C) \((AB)^{-1}=(A+B)^{-1}\)
- D) \((AB)^{-1}=A^{-1}+B^{-1}\)

### Q8
Solve: \(2x+y=5\), \(3x-2y=4\). Value of \(x+y\)?
- A) 1
- B) 2
- C) 3
- D) 4

### Answer Key (with 1-line reasoning)
- Q1: **B** (1·4 − 2·3 = −2)
- Q2: **A** (|AB|=|A||B| = 3·(−2)=−6)
- Q3: **A** (det unchanged by transpose)
- Q4: **A** (det = 6−5=1)
- Q5: **C** (linear dependence → det 0)
- Q6: **A** (right-multiply by diag scales columns)
- Q7: **B**
- Q8: **C** (x=2, y=1 ⇒ x+y=3)
